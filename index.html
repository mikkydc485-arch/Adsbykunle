SECTION 05

Key Performance Indicators

The main metrics a Facebook Ads media buyer may monitor. This section demonstrates understanding of advertising measurement and does not show personal campaign results.

MetricMeaning and formulaWhy it matters
CPMCost per 1,000 impressions.(Ad Spend ÷ Impressions) × 1,000Shows how expensive it is to reach an audience. Influenced by competition, audience and creative quality.
CPCAverage cost of each link click.Ad Spend ÷ Link ClicksMeasures traffic efficiency. A low CPC does not guarantee good conversions.
CTRShare of impressions that led to a link click.(Link Clicks ÷ Impressions) × 100Indicates how relevant and engaging the creative is.
CPAAverage cost to acquire a defined conversion, such as a purchase.Ad Spend ÷ Attributed ConversionsMust be compared with profit margin and customer value.
CPLAverage cost of each lead.Ad Spend ÷ LeadsCore lead-generation metric. Read alongside lead quality.
ROASAttributed revenue earned for each unit of ad spend.Attributed Revenue ÷ Ad SpendShows revenue efficiency. Break-even ROAS depends on margins (1 ÷ profit margin).
Conversion RatePercentage of visitors or clicks that complete the desired action.(Conversions ÷ Link Clicks) × 100Reveals the strength of the landing page, offer and traffic quality.
FrequencyAverage number of times each person saw the ad.Impressions ÷ ReachRising frequency may signal creative fatigue or a small audience.
ReachNumber of unique accounts that saw the ad.Shows the breadth of the audience exposed to the message.
ImpressionsTotal number of times the ad was displayed.The base for CPM and CTR. One person can generate several impressions.
Landing Page ViewsClicks where the destination page fully loaded.LPV Rate = LPV ÷ Link ClicksA large gap versus clicks can indicate slow pages or tracking issues.
Cost per ResultSpend divided by the results the objective optimises for.Ad Spend ÷ ResultsThe main efficiency measure, aligned with the chosen objective.
Add to CartTimes people added a product to their cart after the ad.ATC Rate = Add to Carts ÷ Landing Page ViewsSignals purchase intent and product-page appeal.
Purchase ConversionPercentage of visitors or clicks that complete a purchase.(Purchases ÷ Link Clicks) × 100The key e-commerce indicator of how well the journey turns interest into sales.

Interpret with context. Correct reading of any metric depends on the campaign objective, attribution settings, tracking quality and the economics of the business.

My Advertising Workflow

My proposed working methodology for planning campaigns, from first conversation to reporting. Learning from reporting feeds back into research and creative development.

1Discovery2Research3Strategy4Creative5Setup6Testing7Optimize8Reporting

STAGE 1 · Business Discovery

  • Understand the business
  • Identify the product or service
  • Define business objectives
  • Identify the ideal customer
  • Understand the offer
  • Identify the campaign's intended outcome

STAGE 2 · Market and Audience Research

  • Research potential customers
  • Develop audience profiles
  • Identify customer needs
  • Examine the competitive landscape
  • Research advertising messages
  • Identify potential audience segments

STAGE 3 · Campaign Strategy

  • Select an appropriate objective
  • Map the customer journey
  • Develop a campaign structure
  • Plan audience testing
  • Establish an initial budget framework
  • Define the measurement plan

STAGE 4 · Creative Development

  • Develop ad concepts
  • Write advertising copy
  • Create attention-grabbing hooks
  • Plan video and image creatives
  • Develop calls to action
  • Prepare creative variations

STAGE 5 · Campaign Setup

  • Review the campaign objective
  • Organize campaigns and ad sets
  • Configure targeting
  • Upload creative assets
  • Configure tracking where available
  • Check settings before launch

STAGE 6 · Monitoring and Testing

  • Review campaign delivery
  • Monitor key performance indicators
  • Compare creative performance
  • Evaluate audience performance
  • Identify issues
  • Document observations

STAGE 7 · Optimization

  • Identify areas for improvement
  • Test new creative variations
  • Adjust elements when justified by data
  • Evaluate budget changes carefully
  • Monitor changes over time

STAGE 8 · Reporting

  • Summarize campaign activity
  • Review the relevant metrics
  • Explain observations
  • Document improvements and issues
  • Identify next steps
  • Communicate findings clearly

This is my proposed working methodology. It is not a claim that every step has been used on every campaign.

SECTION 12

Audience Research and Targeting

Audience research connects the business offer to the people most likely to value it. It guides both the targeting and the creative message.

ComponentWhat it meansConsiderations
Customer personasA profile of an ideal customer built from research.Base on real customer data or interviews where possible.
Audience segmentationGrouping potential customers by shared needs or traits.Each segment may respond to a different message.
Customer needsThe problems, goals and desires the offer addresses.Shapes hooks and benefit statements.
Purchase intentHow ready someone is to buy.Cold, warm and hot audiences need different messages.
GeographicWhere customers are and where the business can serve.Align targeting with delivery or service areas.
DemographicAge, language and similar attributes.Treat as hypotheses and avoid stereotypes. Some options are restricted in certain ad categories.
InterestsTopics and behaviours people engage with.Directional signals to test, not certainties.
Existing customer audiencesCustomer lists and past site visitors.Only where lawfully collected and used in line with privacy law and Meta policy.
RetargetingRe-engaging people who already interacted.Requires sufficient audience size and appropriate tracking and consent.
Audience exclusionsRemoving irrelevant groups or existing buyers.Use where justified to reduce wasted spend.
Creative-to-audience alignmentMatching the message and format to the audience and stage.A strong creative in front of the wrong audience will underperform.

Platform targeting options and advertising policies can change. Campaigns must comply with applicable Meta policies and privacy laws.

SECTION 13

Creative Testing and A/B Testing

Testing replaces assumptions with evidence. A good test changes one thing at a time, runs long enough to be meaningful and is documented so the learning is reused.

Different advertising hooks

The first seconds decide whether anyone keeps watching.

Video versus static

Compare formats with the same message.

Product demonstrations

Test showing the product in use against other approaches.

Different calls to action

Wording can change how ready people feel to act.

Different value propositions

Find which benefit matters most to the audience.

Audience and creative alignment

A message may work for one segment and not another.

Test objectives

Define the question and the primary metric before launch.

Test duration

Allow enough time and delivery for a fair read.

Avoiding premature conclusions

Early results are noisy. Avoid judging on a few clicks.

Documenting results

Record the hypothesis, setup, result and next step.

Applying learning

Use findings to shape the next round of creatives.
